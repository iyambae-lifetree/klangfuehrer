Desktop Hi-Fi Setup: How to Match Headphones, DAC and Speakers

You can spot a good headphone and DAC pairing by two numbers: the impedance of the headphones and the output power of the amp. If those match, the rest is mostly a question of connections. A 120-ohm headphone like the Sennheiser HD 560S runs with plenty of headroom on a powerful desktop DAC like the FiiO K11. And if you also want speakers at the same desk, you don't necessarily need a second device.

What makes up a desktop hi-fi setup?

Every playback chain has the same four links, even if some of them live in one box:

  1. The source: your computer, phone or a streamer. This is where the music lives, as a file or a stream.
  2. The DAC (digital-to-analog converter): it turns the numbers into an electrical voltage that behaves like the original sound wave.
  3. The amplifier: it brings that voltage up to the level your headphones or speakers need.
  4. The transducer: headphones or speakers that turn electricity back into moving air.

Your laptop already has a DAC and an amp built in, just small ones designed to save power. A desktop DAC like the FiiO K11 puts the converter and headphone amp in one box. Powered speakers like the Edifier R1280DBs bring their own amp and, through their digital inputs, even their own DAC. So the question "what goes together?" really means: which links do you already have, and which one is the weakest?

Impedance, sensitivity, power: the three numbers that matter

Impedance is the headphones' AC resistance, measured in ohms (Ω). Roughly speaking, the higher it is, the more voltage the headphones need to reach the same volume.

Sensitivity tells you how loud headphones get at a given voltage. According to Sennheiser, the HD 560S reaches 110 dB at 1 volt. That's plenty for normal listening levels, so an amp isn't strictly necessary for it. It does benefit from a small one, though, because a laptop output quickly runs out of room with quiet recordings.

Output power is what the amp can deliver, usually listed in milliwatts into a given load. According to FiiO, the K11 delivers 1400 mW into 32 Ω from its balanced output. The Beyerdynamic DT 990 Pro uses the single-ended output because of its fixed cable, and according to German site hifi.de that output is enough for 250 Ω too. For that headphone, the manufacturer says an amp really is necessary. The reasons are in Does the DT 990 Pro 250 ohm need an amp?.

What about bit depth and sample rate?

The sample rate tells you how many times per second the signal is captured (CD: 44.1 kHz). The bit depth tells you how finely each sample is resolved (CD: 16-bit). Practically every current DAC goes beyond CD quality. The K11, for example, handles PCM up to 32-bit/384 kHz and DSD256. What matters more is that the file actually arrives that way: the K11's display shows the incoming resolution. If it says 48 kHz while you're playing a 96 kHz file, your operating system is resampling. On a Mac, you set this in Audio MIDI Setup.

How to find the right combination: the decision rules

  • If you only use speakers at your desk, you don't need an external DAC, as long as the speakers have a digital input. The R1280DBs accepts your computer over optical or coaxial.
  • If your headphones are under about 50 Ω and get loud enough on your laptop, start with what you have. An external DAC will then mainly fix hiss and hum, not a lack of power.
  • If your headphones are 120 Ω like the HD 560S, your existing output is often enough, but a desktop DAC gives you headroom and a quieter background.
  • If your headphones are 250 Ω or more, plan on an amp. Here power matters, not the converter chip.
  • If you want headphones and speakers at the same desk, get a DAC with a line output. The K11 has an RCA output where you can connect powered speakers, such as the R1280DBs or the analog-only R1280T.
  • If you have or want a balanced cable, look for a 4.4 mm output. Balanced means each side is driven separately with its own counterpart, which gives some amps more power.

FAQ

Do I need to buy my DAC and headphones from the same brand?

No. The connectors are standardized (1/4-inch, 3.5 mm, 4.4 mm), and there's no brand lock-in. What matters is impedance, sensitivity and power, not the logo.

Can an amp be too powerful for my headphones?

Power you don't use does no harm. Just be careful when powering on and with the volume knob: start low. With very sensitive headphones, a powerful amp can also leave you little usable range on the volume knob at low levels.

Does an external DAC make any difference on a Mac?

That depends on your headphones and your ears. With easy-going headphones, the difference is often small. With high-impedance headphones or noise on the output, it's clear. The full answer is in Is an external DAC worth it on a Mac or PC?.

Do I need an expensive cable?

No. An intact cable of the right length is enough. Your money is better spent on headphones or an amp.
