Is an External DAC Worth It for Your Mac or PC?

Is an external DAC worth it? How to tell if your Mac or PC needs one, what it audibly improves, and when you're better off keeping your money.

An external DAC is worth it when your computer has a specific problem: the headphone output hisses or hums, it can't get loud enough for your headphones, or you're missing connections. If your headphones already sound clean and loud enough on your Mac or PC, an external converter won't transform the sound. In that case, the money is usually better spent on better headphones.

We did not measure the devices mentioned here ourselves. The information comes from expert reviews, measurement sites and manufacturer specs. More on that in How we choose.

What does a DAC actually do?

Every music file and every stream is stored digitally, as a long series of numbers. To turn that into sound, a DAC (digital-to-analog converter) has to translate those numbers into an electrical voltage, which then drives headphones or an amplifier. So every computer with a headphone jack already has a DAC built in, plus a small headphone amp that brings the signal up to volume.

An external DAC moves both of those out of the computer and into a separate box. The music leaves the computer digitally over USB, and only the external device converts and amplifies it. That's why almost every device in this class is really a DAC and headphone amp in one.

How to tell your computer isn't enough

Three signs are clear-cut; a fourth is more a matter of taste.

1. It hisses or hums. Inside a computer, the power supply, graphics card and processor all work in a tight space. That can show up as faint hiss, whine, or noise that changes when you move the mouse or the system is under load. You'll hear it most in quiet passages and with sensitive in-ear monitors. An external DAC moves the conversion away from that environment, and this is its most convincing benefit.

2. It's too quiet. Your headphones are what decide this. Their impedance (electrical resistance, in ohms) and sensitivity (how loud they play at a given voltage) determine how much amplification they need. A Sennheiser HD 599 SE at 50 Ω runs fine straight from a laptop. A Sennheiser HD 560S at 120 Ω reaches 110 dB at one volt according to Sennheiser; it doesn't strictly need an amp, but it benefits from one. A beyerdynamic DT 990 PRO at 250 Ω needs an amp according to beyerdynamic. If you turn your computer's volume all the way up and it's still not loud enough, you're short on power, and an external device supplies it. We walk through an example in detail in DT 990 Pro 250 ohm: do you need an amp?

3. You're missing connections. Many laptops only have a combined headset jack, and some desktop PCs have none that's easy to reach. An external DAC adds a full-size 1/4-inch (6.35 mm) jack, often a balanced 4.4 mm output, and an RCA output for powered speakers. Some also accept a TV or game console through an optical input.

4. You want more control. A display showing the current sample rate, a built-in equalizer, a real volume knob: that's convenience, not a sonic miracle. For many people it's still a good reason.

What an external DAC can't do

Being honest here saves you money.

  • It won't make bad headphones good. The biggest difference in sound in any setup comes from the headphones or speakers, not the converter. If you only have one budget, spend it there first. For ideas, see our guide to the best open-back headphones under $200.
  • It won't turn an MP3 into hi-res. What was lost in compression doesn't come back.
  • It does nothing for Bluetooth headphones. Wireless headphones have their own built-in converter. A USB DAC on your computer is simply left out of the chain.
  • More kilohertz doesn't automatically mean better sound. The sample rate is how many times per second the signal is captured; the bit depth is how finely each value is resolved. CD quality (16-bit, 44.1 kHz) already covers the range of human hearing. 24-bit hi-res files mainly offer more headroom for recording and editing. Accepting 32-bit/384 kHz is now standard and no longer a reason to buy a particular device.

The decision rule

  • If your computer hisses, hums or whines: then an external DAC is almost always worth it. Even a small device usually fixes that.
  • If your headphones are high-impedance (around 250 Ω, like the DT 990 PRO) or too quiet on your computer: then you need a device with a strong amp, not a dongle.
  • If you listen with IEMs or easy-to-drive headphones on a laptop and the jack is just missing or noisy: then a small USB-C dongle is enough.
  • If you run powered speakers and headphones at the same desk: then pick a desktop unit with an RCA output.
  • If everything sounds clean and loud enough: then skip the DAC and put the money toward headphones, speakers or music.

Mac or PC: is there a difference?

Basically no. Both send music digitally over USB to an external converter. There are still two practical points.

On a Mac, you set the sample rate and bit depth the output device runs at in the Audio MIDI Setup app. On a Windows PC, you'll find the setting in the advanced properties of the playback device. A DAC with a display shows you what's actually arriving, which helps you catch wrong settings.

Before you buy, check whether the device needs a particular kind of computer for firmware updates. FiiO says firmware updates for the K11 only work on Windows. That doesn't affect playback on a Mac, but if you only own a Mac, you should know.

FAQ

Does an external DAC sound noticeably better than the built-in one?

If the built-in output hisses or lacks power: yes, clearly. If it works cleanly and your headphones are easy to drive, the differences are small.

Is a dongle enough, or do I need a desktop unit?

A dongle is enough for IEMs and low-impedance headphones. For high-impedance headphones, multiple sources or powered speakers, a desktop unit is the better choice.

Is a DAC worth it for regular-quality streaming?

If your computer hisses or is too quiet, yes. That problem exists regardless of the source. For hi-res, though, the source comes first: without lossless files, even the best DAC can't bring anything back.

Do I also need a separate headphone amp?

Usually not. Devices in this class have a headphone amp built in, and the desktop models have enough power even for 250 Ω headphones.

Start by listening closely to what bothers you about your current sound. That tells you whether a DAC will help, and which one.
