DT 990 Pro 250 Ohm: Do You Need an Amp?

Can you run the DT 990 Pro 250 ohm without an amp? On a phone it often stays too quiet. When you need an amp, which one is enough, and why impedance matters.

Yes, in most cases the 250 ohm version of the beyerdynamic DT 990 Pro needs an amp. beyerdynamic itself lists a headphone amp or an audio interface as a requirement, and according to reviews it often stays too quiet on a phone or a basic laptop headphone jack. A desktop DAC with a built-in headphone amp, like the FiiO K11, solves the problem. A small dongle is more of a stopgap.

Why does a 250 ohm headphone need more voltage?

Impedance is the electrical resistance the headphone puts up against the music signal, measured in ohms (Ω). The power that reaches the headphone comes from voltage and resistance: power = voltage² ÷ resistance. Put the other way around: at the same voltage, a 250 Ω headphone gets only about one eighth of the power a 32 Ω headphone would get.

How loud that actually gets depends on the headphone's sensitivity, meaning the sound pressure it produces at a given voltage or power. For the DT 990 Pro we couldn't find a reliable figure from an independent source. The manufacturer's statement is clear, though, and it matches what reviewers report.

The headphone jack on phones and laptops is usually designed for low-impedance earbuds. There, a little voltage gives you plenty of volume. At 250 Ω, that kind of output hits its limit.

What happens if you run the DT 990 Pro without an amp?

Nothing breaks. A weak output doesn't harm the headphone. You notice it in other ways: you turn the volume way up or all the way, quiet recordings still stay too quiet, and there's no headroom for loud passages. With classical music and jazz, which have big jumps in volume, this shows up more than with loudly mastered pop.

A quick check with your own device settles it: play a quiet track, say a solo piano recording, at about three quarters of the volume. If that's already too quiet, you need an amp.

Reading power specs: why 1400 mW isn't always 1400 mW

Manufacturers almost always rate headphone amp output into a low load, usually 32 Ω, and often 16 Ω for dongles. According to its spec sheet, the FiiO K11 delivers 1400 milliwatts into 32 Ω from its balanced 4.4 mm output. For the single-ended output, which the DT 990 Pro uses with its fixed cable, FiiO lists 520 mW into 32 Ω and 60 mW into 300 Ω. The Moondrop Dawn Pro 2 is rated at 124 milliwatts into 16 Ω. Into 250 Ω, both deliver much less, because there the output's maximum voltage sets the limit, not its power.

That's why the milliwatt number alone doesn't tell you much. For the K11 we have an independent assessment: German site hifi.de says it has enough power even for 250 Ω headphones. For the Dawn Pro 2 there's no measurement into 250 Ω.

Important with the DT 990 Pro: the cable is permanently attached. You can't use balanced 4.4 mm outputs, where many amps and dongles deliver their full power, without modifying the headphone. What counts is the regular single-ended output.

Is an audio interface enough?

If you record music, you might already have an audio interface on your desk, meaning an external box with mic and instrument inputs and its own headphone output. beyerdynamic explicitly names it as an alternative to a headphone amp. How strong your interface's headphone output is, though, depends a lot on the model. Try the quick check above before you buy anything.

The decision rule

  • If you already own the DT 990 Pro and listen at your computer, get a desktop DAC with a headphone amp. That's the fix that reliably solves the problem.
  • If you own an audio interface, first test its headphone output with the quick check. If it's enough, you don't need anything else.
  • If you mostly listen on your phone or on the go, an open-back 250 Ω headphone is the wrong choice. A dongle makes it louder, but not a good fit. An easy-to-drive model like the Sennheiser HD 599 SE (50 Ω) suits you better.
  • If you haven't bought yet and don't want an amp, pick a low-impedance model. beyerdynamic also makes an 80 Ω version, but we haven't evaluated it separately.
  • If you haven't bought yet and want the DT 990 Pro for its sense of space, budget for the amp from the start. Headphone plus K11 together land well above $200. At that point it's worth checking our best open-back headphones under $200, because the Sennheiser HD 560S usually works without extra gear.

FAQ

Can I use the DT 990 Pro 250 ohm with my phone?

It works, you'll hear something. According to reviews, though, it often doesn't get loud enough, especially with quiet recordings. For listening on a phone, a low-impedance headphone is the better choice.

Can too little power damage the headphone?

No. A weak output just makes the headphone quieter and takes away its headroom. If anything, watch out for your hearing when a powerful amp is involved: turn the volume down before you put the headphones on.

Is the headphone jack on my Mac or PC enough?

It depends on the device, and manufacturers rarely publish the numbers. Do the quick check with a quiet recording. Whether an external DAC is worth it for other reasons too, we cover in Is an external DAC worth it?.

What's the difference from the 80 ohm version?

The 80 Ω version needs less voltage for the same volume, so it's less demanding on weak outputs. Beyond that we haven't evaluated it, which is why we don't compare their sound here.

Can I connect the DT 990 Pro balanced?

Not without modifying it, because the cable is permanently attached. That's why an amp with a strong single-ended output matters more than a 4.4 mm jack.

An amp won't turn the DT 990 Pro into a different headphone, but only with one do you hear what you bought it for.
