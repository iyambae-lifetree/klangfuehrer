What matters in a DAC/headphone amp

A DAC (digital-to-analog converter) turns the ones and zeros of your music file into an analog signal. The headphone amp behind it brings that signal up to the voltage and current your headphones need. Every device in this comparison has both in one box.

Power that matches your headphones. What you plug in is what decides this. Impedance (the headphone's electrical resistance, in ohms) and sensitivity (how loud it plays at a given voltage) determine how much amplification you need. A Sennheiser HD 599 SE at 50 Ω runs from almost anything. A beyerdynamic DT 990 PRO at 250 Ω needs an amp according to beyerdynamic, and this is where a strong desktop unit pulls away from a dongle. We go into the details in DT 990 Pro 250 ohm: do you need an amp?.

The power figures on spec sheets come from the manufacturers and are usually measured at the balanced output into 32 Ω. They're fine as a rough ranking, but don't compare them down to the last milliwatt.

Balanced output. Almost every device here has a 4.4 mm (Pentaconn) jack next to the regular headphone jack. Balanced means each channel gets its own ground wire, and the amp can deliver more voltage. You can only use it with a matching cable, which many headphones don't include.

Inputs. USB alone is enough if only your computer plays music. An optical input (Toslink) also lets you route a TV, game console or CD player to your headphones.

Sample rate and bit depth. All five handle PCM up to 32-bit/384 kHz and DSD256. The sample rate is how many times per second the signal is captured; the bit depth is how finely each sample is resolved. Any of these devices covers hi-res files from Qobuz or Tidal (usually 24-bit, up to 192 kHz). In this class, it's no longer something that sets products apart.

Line output. An RCA output turns the DAC into a source for powered desktop speakers as well.

FiiO K11: why it comes out on top

The K11 combines the most power in this group (1400 mW into 32 Ω balanced and 520 mW single-ended, per the spec sheet) with USB, optical and coaxial inputs. That's enough to drive 250 Ω headphones with confidence. German site hifi.de gives it 8.5 out of 10. The display that shows the incoming resolution is more than a gimmick: you can see right away whether your computer passes a hi-res file through untouched or resamples it first. There are two catches. It has no analog input, and firmware updates only work on Windows. If you only own a Mac, you'll need to borrow a Windows PC for that.

Topping DX1 II: the all-rounder

The DX1 II does the most jobs: optical in and out, a 10-band parametric equalizer (handy for taming, say, a sharp treble peak in your headphones), and a headset input with a mic for gaming and video calls. What Hi-Fi describes its sound as very assured and undramatic. Honestly, that's more a matter of character than a flaw. If you want a converter that adds nothing, this is a good fit. Listings come from several third-party sellers, some with "Gaming" in the title, so check who you're buying from.

FiiO K11 R2R: the different one

Most DACs use a delta-sigma chip: it converts at a very high clock rate with few steps and filters the result digitally. An R2R converter instead turns each sample directly into a voltage through a network of precise resistors. The K11 R2R offers this at a price where it's rarely found, and lets you switch between NOS (non-oversampling, so no digital filter) and OS (oversampling). In NOS mode the very highest frequencies roll off slightly with CD material, which some people like. Whether you hear a difference is something you can find out for yourself here. We didn't find a rating from the German hi-fi press, and buyer feedback is mixed. Also, the optical input only accepts up to 96 kHz.

iFi Zen DAC V2: the established choice

The Zen DAC V2 has a good reputation (hifi.de Editors' Choice 2023), and thanks to its volume knob it also works as a preamp for powered speakers. But it only has a USB input, and according to iFi's spec sheet it puts out noticeably less power than the three units above: over 380 mW into 50 Ω balanced and over 280 mW into 32 Ω single-ended. Its successor, the Zen DAC 3, is above our budget. The V2 is remaining stock, so availability could end at any time.

Moondrop Dawn Pro 2: small, with limits

The Dawn Pro 2 isn't a desktop unit but a USB-C dongle, and it still belongs on this list: if you listen with IEMs or easy-to-drive headphones, you simply don't need more. Two Cirrus Logic DAC chips, a balanced 4.4 mm output and an EQ you set via app or browser are a lot for the money. That said, the measurement site audioreview.frieve.com rates its value as only below average and reports wobbly buttons. For high-impedance headphones it's on the weak side at the 3.5 mm output, and we don't know of a measurement into 250 Ω.

Which DAC is right for you?

  • You have high-impedance headphones like the DT 990 PRO (250 Ω): FiiO K11, Topping DX1 II or K11 R2R. All three have headroom; the Dawn Pro 2 doesn't.
  • Your desk also has a console, TV or CD player: Topping DX1 II or FiiO K11, both with an optical input.
  • You want to fine-tune your headphones' sound: Topping DX1 II with its 10-band EQ. On the go, the Dawn Pro 2 can do that too.
  • You only listen at your computer and also want to control powered speakers: iFi Zen DAC V2, while it's still available.
  • You're curious whether converter design is audible: FiiO K11 R2R with its NOS/OS switch.
  • You listen with IEMs on your phone or laptop: Moondrop Dawn Pro 2. A desktop unit would be money spent on power you'll never use.
  • Your computer's headphone jack doesn't hiss and gets loud enough: You may not need anything. When an external DAC actually pays off is covered in Is an external DAC worth it?.

FAQ

Do I need a DAC if my headphones are loud enough on my laptop?

Not necessarily. An external DAC mainly helps when the built-in output hisses, hums, is too quiet, or you're missing connections. If everything sounds clean and loud enough, a device from this list is an upgrade, not a necessity.

What does the balanced 4.4 mm output do?

It gives you more voltage headroom and separate ground wires for each channel. With high-impedance headphones, that means more room to spare. You need a matching balanced cable, and your headphones need a detachable cable. On the DT 990 PRO, the cable is permanently attached.

Can you hear the difference between R2R and delta-sigma?

There's no blanket answer. Both approaches can work very cleanly. That's exactly why the FiiO K11 R2R is interesting: with its NOS/OS switch you can compare for yourself instead of relying on someone else's impressions.

Can I use it to run speakers?

Yes, via the RCA output into powered speakers or an amplifier. The FiiO K11, K11 R2R, Topping DX1 II and iFi Zen DAC V2 have one; the Dawn Pro 2 doesn't.

In the end, your headphones decide: buy as much amp as they need, and put the rest toward good music.
