What matters with open-back headphones

Open-back vs. semi-open: what it means day to day

Open-back headphones have ear cups that let sound pass through to the outside, usually behind a grille. Sound doesn't get trapped in a closed chamber, which is why many listeners describe the sound as airier and less "inside your head." The downside isn't negotiable: people around you hear what you're listening to, and you hear them. A semi-open model like the Superlux HD681 sits in between, but it barely isolates either.

That makes open-back headphones a choice for a quiet room at home. On a train, in an open office, or in a bedroom with a sleeping partner, they're the wrong design, no matter how good they sound.

Impedance and sensitivity: do you need an amp?

Impedance is the headphone's electrical resistance in ohms (Ω). It tells you how much voltage a source has to deliver. The higher it is, the more voltage you need for the same volume. Sensitivity, meaning how loud a headphone gets at a given input voltage, is the other half: Sennheiser rates the HD 560S at 110 dB at 1 volt, for example.

The models on this list range from 30 Ω (Philips Fidelio X2HR) to 250 Ω (beyerdynamic DT 990 Pro). A rule of thumb for these five:

  • 30 to 50 Ω (X2HR, HD681, HD 599 SE): a laptop, phone or small dongle is enough.
  • 120 Ω (HD 560S): works with most devices, but a small amp gives you headroom.
  • 250 Ω (DT 990 Pro): according to the manufacturer, it needs a headphone amp or an audio interface.

We explain what that means in practice, and which amp is enough for 250 Ω, in DT 990 Pro 250 ohm: do you need an amp?.

Weight and pads: what counts for long listening sessions

Four of the five models weigh between 230 and 250 g (about 8 to 9 oz). The Fidelio X2HR stands out at around 380 g (13.4 oz). The pads matter at least as much as the weight: the DT 990 Pro and X2HR use velour, the HD681 uses faux leather, which owners say can have sharp edges. The large pads on the HD 599 SE are known for being very comfortable, but they attract dust.

Detachable cable: why it matters more than it sounds

The cable is usually the first thing to break. On the HD 560S, HD 599 SE and X2HR you can simply swap it. On the DT 990 Pro and HD681 it's permanently attached, so a broken cable means a repair.

Tuning: neutral, warm or bright?

No headphone sounds "correct." Every one is tuned. The HD 560S is considered fairly neutral and analytical, the X2HR full and warm. The DT 990 Pro and HD681 emphasize the treble. That brings out detail, but it can get harsh on "s" sounds and hi-hats. If you're sensitive to that, it matters more than any line on a spec sheet.

Sennheiser HD 560S: why it's the all-rounder

The HD 560S sits at the top of this budget, and in return it's the most balanced package. German site hifi.de gives it 8.5 out of 10, and reviewers and owners alike point to its value for the money. Sennheiser specifies distortion (the share of added overtones that aren't in the original recording) at under 0.05 percent at 90 dB. That's a manufacturer figure, not an independent measurement, but it fits the reports of a detailed sound that doesn't tire you out quickly. At 120 Ω it runs on most devices. If you notice you're turning your laptop volume way up, a small USB DAC with headphone amp is the sensible add-on. No carrying case is included.

Sennheiser HD 599 SE: when you don't want extra gear

At 50 Ω, the HD 599 SE is the easiest Sennheiser on this list to drive: plugged straight into a laptop or a dongle, it gets loud enough. German site ocinside describes it as analytical, with more bass than the HD 560S, and a good choice for rock. According to the same source, though, it doesn't reach the level of the HD 600 for vocals and sense of space. A practical detail: the box includes two cables, a 3 m (10 ft) one for the armchair and a 1.2 m (4 ft) one for the desk.

beyerdynamic DT 990 Pro (250 Ω): strong, but only with an amp

The DT 990 Pro is an open-back studio headphone, solidly built, with comfortable velour pads. hifi.de gives it 8.4 out of 10 and highlights its sense of space and detail. Two things you should know before buying: the treble can sound harsh at times, and according to beyerdynamic the 250 Ω version needs a headphone amp or an audio interface. If you already own one, you get a lot of headphone for the money. If you'd have to buy an amp first, add up the total cost. The cable is a permanently attached coiled cable.

Philips Fidelio X2HR: fullness over strictness

The X2HR uses large 50 mm drivers and velour pads with memory foam. Reports describe a big, lively sound with rich mids. At 30 Ω it's very easy to drive. The weaknesses: it weighs around 380 g (Philips says 380, German site kopfhoerer.de weighed 383 g), and kopfhoerer.de criticizes somewhat sharp treble plus minor build-quality flaws. Its price also swings more than the other models'. At the low end of its range it's much more attractive than at the high end.

Superlux HD681: open sound for very little money

The HD681 is the entry point if you want to find out whether you even like an open sound. kopfhoerer.de gives it 3.5 out of 5 and calls it detailed. The treble is clearly emphasized and can sound metallic, the faux leather has sharp edges, and the 2.5 m (8 ft) cable is permanently attached. As your main headphone for years it's a compromise. As a second pair or a first step, it's honestly good.

FAQ

Do I need an amp for open-back headphones?

Not in general. It depends on impedance and sensitivity, not on whether the design is open or closed. The HD 599 SE, Fidelio X2HR and HD681 work without one, and the HD 560S usually does too. Only the 250 Ω DT 990 Pro needs an amp, according to its manufacturer.

Sennheiser HD 560S or HD 599 SE: which is better?

The HD 560S is more neutral and detailed. The HD 599 SE is more comfortable, a bit stronger in the bass and easier to drive. If you listen analytically and don't mind adding a small amp if needed, get the HD 560S. If you don't want to buy anything else and comfort comes first, get the HD 599 SE.

Can I use open-back headphones at the office or on the go?

Technically yes, but it rarely makes sense. Coworkers hear your music, and outside noise reaches you almost unfiltered. Open-back headphones show their strengths in quiet rooms.

Is an external DAC worth it with headphones under $200?

For the 250 Ω DT 990 Pro, yes, because it often stays too quiet without an amp. For the other models it's not a must. When it's still worth it, read Is an external DAC worth it?.

What's the difference between open-back and semi-open?

A semi-open headphone lets less sound through than an open-back one, but it isolates much less than a closed-back one. In daily use it behaves more like an open-back: it's not a good fit for noisy places either.

If you take away just one thing: buy the headphone that fits where you listen and what you plug it into, not the one with the biggest numbers.
