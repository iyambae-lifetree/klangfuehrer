“Most of you don't need a DAC”

On wireless earbuds a DAC does nothing. Wired headphones on a laptop that hisses are a different story. Here are two small ones that fix exactly that. The whole story is in Is an external DAC worth it?

Moondrop Dawn Pro 2

around $50–60 · USB-C dongle DAC

Small USB-C dongle with two Cirrus Logic DAC chips and a balanced 4.4 mm output. Made for IEMs and easy-to-drive headphones on a phone or laptop.

Pros

  • balanced 4.4 mm output at this price
  • excellent manufacturer specs for distortion and dynamic range
  • parametric EQ via app or browser

Cons

  • reports of wobbly buttons and unintended double clicks
  • tight on power for high-impedance headphones on the 3.5 mm output; no measurement at 250 Ω known
  • only a one-year manufacturer warranty (according to audioreview.frieve.com)

“Go look in your parents' attic”

Old passive bookshelf speakers only need a small amp. Want to play straight from your phone? The first one has Bluetooth built in. Already have a streamer? Then the second one is enough. To add streaming to a whole old stereo, read Bluetooth and Wi-Fi for an old stereo.

Fosi Audio BT20A Pro

around $100 · Stereo amplifier with Bluetooth

Small integrated amp for passive speakers, with Bluetooth and tone controls. Brings old bookshelf speakers back to life with nothing else needed.

Pros

  • Bluetooth and RCA input in one box
  • bass and treble knobs with a center detent
  • power supply included, rated “very good” by Audio Science Review

Cons

  • Bluetooth is SBC and AAC only

Before you buy any headphones

Walt's two checks: wear them for an hour, not five minutes. And make sure you can replace the ear pads. If not, walk away.
